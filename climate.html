05 / Climate Controls, Biomes & Climate Change

Life shaped by wind, water, and temperature

The climate of Patagonia is influenced by its high southern latitude, the Andes, the surrounding oceans, elevation, and prevailing winds. Conditions can change over short distances: western areas are generally wetter, while parts of the eastern steppe are much drier.

Understanding the process

A biome is a large ecological region shaped by climate and characteristic plant and animal communities. In and around Torres del Paine, CONAF describes several vegetation environments, including Patagonian steppe, pre-Andean shrubland, deciduous and mixed forests, and high-elevation Andean desert.

Why it matters in Patagonia

These environments support wildlife such as guanacos, pumas, foxes, condors, and many bird species. Plants and animals have adaptations that help them survive local conditions, including strong winds, cold temperatures, and differences in water availability.

Climate change can affect glaciers, snow cover, water availability, habitats, and the timing of seasonal events. Observing these changes matters because ecosystems and people are connected. Conservation, careful tourism, and scientific monitoring can help protect Patagonia's natural heritage.

Climate controls

Latitude · Elevation · Ocean influence · Prevailing winds · Relief

The Andes influence how air moves and where precipitation falls. Mountain barriers can create wetter conditions on one side and drier conditions on the other, contributing to contrasts between forest and steppe.
