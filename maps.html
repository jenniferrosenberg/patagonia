01 / Maps, Location & Cartographers

Finding Patagonia on the map

Patagonia is a large geographic region shared by southern Chile and Argentina. This project focuses on the Chilean side, especially the Magallanes Region and Torres del Paine National Park, in the far south of the country.

Understanding the process

Maps help us understand where places are, how far apart they are, and how physical features are connected. A physical map can show mountain ranges, rivers, lakes, and elevation. A political map shows boundaries and administrative regions. Satellite images add another perspective by showing patterns on Earth's surface.

Why it matters in Patagonia

Torres del Paine National Park is in the Province of Última Esperanza, Magallanes and Chilean Antarctic Region. CONAF lists the park's area as about 227,298 hectares. Its location includes mountain terrain, glaciers, lakes, rivers, and steppe landscapes.

When reading a map of Patagonia, look for the Andes, the Southern Patagonian Ice Field, fjords, and the coast. Compare a physical map with a satellite image: each one reveals different information about the same landscape.

Map reading

Scale, direction, legend, latitude, longitude, elevation

A map is a model of a real place. Scale tells us the relationship between map distance and ground distance; a legend explains symbols; and coordinates help identify a location precisely.
