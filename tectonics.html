02 / Plate Tectonics, Earthquakes & Volcanoes

A restless edge of the continent

The Andes are connected to the movement of tectonic plates along the western edge of South America. Along much of Chile, the oceanic Nazca Plate moves beneath the South American Plate. This process is called subduction.

Understanding the process

Subduction helps explain why the Andes are high and why the region experiences earthquakes and volcanic activity. As plates converge, stress can build along locked sections of a fault. When rocks suddenly slip, seismic waves travel through the Earth and we feel an earthquake.

Why it matters in Patagonia

Southern Chile is geologically complex. Near the far south, the interactions among the South American, Antarctic, and Scotia plates also influence the region. The USGS describes these plate boundaries and their relationship to earthquakes and deformation.

The mountains we see are part of a much larger system that is still moving. Plate tectonics works over long periods, but earthquakes remind us that the Earth is active today. Understanding these processes also helps communities prepare for natural hazards.

What is subduction?

Convergence · Oceanic plate · Continental plate · Mantle

At a convergent boundary, one tectonic plate can descend beneath another. In the Peru–Chile trench region, the Nazca Plate is subducted beneath South America. This process contributes to mountain building, earthquakes, and volcanism.
